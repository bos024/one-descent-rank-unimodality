\paragraph{An elementary mode-only convolution theorem.}
Let $g$ be nonnegative, finite, unimodal with interval support, and
first mode $M$. Assume LC only at internal centres $n<M$.
Then $B=[t]g$, $t\ge1$, is unimodal and LC at every centre strictly
before its FIRST mode. No LC in the descending part is needed.

For centres $n<M$, truncate $g$ above $M$. This is globally LC
(the endpoint minor is automatic), and its interval convolution
agrees with the full triple at $n$, so ordinary LC convolution pays it.
For $M\le n\le M+t-1$, the exact second difference is
\[
 B_{n+1}-2B_n+B_{n-1}
  =(g_{n+1}-g_n)-(g_{n+1-t}-g_{n-t})\le0.
\]
The entering pair is in the descending prefix after $M$; the outgoing
pair is in the nondecreasing prefix up to $M$. Zero indices are allowed.
Thus $B$ is concave throughout the whole transition range, and
\[
 B_n^2-B_{n-1}B_{n+1}
 =(B_n-B_{n-1})^2+B_{n-1}(2B_n-B_{n-1}-B_{n+1})\ge0.
\]
Before $M$ its increments are nonnegative; after $M+t-1$ they are
nonpositive; in between the increments are nonincreasing. This also
proves unimodality and puts its first mode at most $M+t-1$.
At its first mode the ordinary minor is automatic. This is a complete
written proof of mode-only LC preservation, not merely a numerical guess.
