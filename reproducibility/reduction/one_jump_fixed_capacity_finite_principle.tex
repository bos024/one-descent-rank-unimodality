\section{A finite principle for complete one-jump C at every fixed capacity}
Fix $L\ge3$ and put $H=L+2$.  Let
\[
 F_{h,L}=F_{1^\alpha2^hL},\quad
 D_{h,L}(n)=(F_{h,L}(n)-F_{h,L}(n-1))^+\quad(n\ge2),
 \qquad d_h(j)=D_{h,L}(j+2).
\]
The padding $\alpha\ge1$ remains arbitrary throughout the propagation.
The reference is the already proved complete-C sequence
\[
 r_n=\operatorname{Fib}(n)-\operatorname{Fib}(n-L),\quad n\ge2,
 \qquad a_j=r_{j+2};                                    \tag{1}
\]
negative Fibonacci indices mean zero.  It comes from
$1+(z+\cdots+z^L)/(1-z-z^2)$.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-capacity whole-generation finite principle]
For any fixed integer $L\ge3$, the following finite conditions suffice
for complete property C of EVERY actual arm $1^\alpha2^hL$,
with no bound on $h\ge1$ or $\alpha\ge1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Complete C holds for $1\le h\le H$ and
      $1\le\alpha\le h+L$.
\item At $h=H$, for $1\le\alpha\le H+L$, the ENTIRE
      positive-slope tail is log-concave at every centre
      $n\ge H+2$, including after its first slope mode.
\end{enumerate}
Both tests need only ordinary coefficients through $2h+L+1$.
This is a sufficient finite criterion at EACH capacity, not an
assumption that a finite capacity cutoff covers all capacities.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Exact coverage of the initial padding and rank domains}
The first ordinary mode of a binary arm is at most $2h$.
Appending $L$ gives first ordinary mode at most $2h+L$:
past that rank the exact difference
$F_h(n-1)-F_h(n-L-1)$ samples two old indices on the
nonincreasing tail in their correct order.  Actual ordinary
unimodality therefore puts the entire positive slope support in
$[2,2h+L]$.  The extra read at $2h+L+1$ includes its zero successor.

For all $h$, the exact binary polynomial is
\[
 F_h=\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}(z+z^2)^j+(z+z^2)^h[\alpha+1].     \tag{2}
\]
Increasing initial padding past $h+L$ first changes $F_h$ at
rank at least $2h+L+1$, and first changes the appended
$F_{h,L}$ at rank at least $2h+L+2$.
Consequently clamping $\alpha$ to $h+L$ preserves ALL required
coefficients through $2h+L+1$ and hence the whole positive-D sequence,
its modes, all C reads, and the full-tail LC entrance.
This is proved local agreement plus an independent mode bound;
it is not a mode-preserving assertion about arbitrary clipping.

\subsection*{Unbounded propagation of the FULL slope tail}
For fixed $L$, the exact generating identities are
\[
 F_{h+1,L}=(z+z^2)F_{h,L}+1+z^3+\cdots+z^L,
\]
and, for the signed slopes shifted down by two,
\[
 \widetilde d_{h+1}
        =(z+z^2)\widetilde d_h+1+z-z^{L-1}.             \tag{3}
\]
Both actual words remain sorted; (3) changes the number of twos
BEFORE the fixed last capacity, and is an exact polynomial identity,
not a legal append of a two after $L$.
The ordinary binary exact prefix gives
\[
 d_h(j)=a_j\quad(j\le h),\qquad
                          d_h(h+1)\le a_{h+1}-1.         \tag{4}
\]
The positive support is an integer interval $[0,J_h]$ with $J_h\ge h$.
For $h\ge H$, the fixed forcing in (3) lies strictly before every
tail read.  Hence $J_{h+1}\le J_h+2$, the new tail equals the
two-term window through $J_h+1$, and only its last possible
coefficient at $J_h+2$ may be reduced or deleted.  Signed old slopes
past $J_h$ are nonpositive, so this covers every tail coefficient.

Let
\[
 C_L=\operatorname{Fib}(L-1)+\operatorname{Fib}(L+1)
                              -1-(-1)^L.
\]
The proved Fibonacci recurrence gives reference minors of magnitude
$C_L$ in its homogeneous range.  Moreover
\[
 r_{L+2}-C_L=\operatorname{Fib}(L-2)+(-1)^L\ge0,
                  \qquad r_n\ge C_L\quad(n\ge L+2).     \tag{5}
\]
The reference is increasing there.  Thus for $h\ge H$,
\[
       a_h^2-a_{h-1}(a_{h+1}-1)\ge-C_L+r_{h+1}\ge0.   \tag{6}
\]

Induct on $h\ge H$ to prove LC of the ENTIRE $d_h$ tail
at centres $j\ge h$.  The base is finite condition 2.
For $d_{h+1}$ at its first required centre $h+1$, use
the two exact prefix values, the first defect (4), and (6).
At every later interior centre, the two relevant old centres
are in its already proved whole tail.  The two-term window
minor is the sum of the two old minors and their nonnegative
cross minor, as in the preceding ordinary-binary proof.
Reducing a final coefficient improves its preceding minor;
its own terminal minor is its square.  Zero extension gives
all remaining centres.  This proves, for every $h\ge H$,
\[
 D_{h,L}(n)^2\ge D_{h,L}(n-1)D_{h,L}(n+1)
                               \quad(n\ge h+2),        \tag{7}
over the WHOLE positive-slope tail, not only its rising part.

\subsection*{From the propagated tail to EVERY C shift}
Fix $h\ge H$ and suppress the subscripts.
Through $N\le h+2$, the actual slopes and cumulatives agree
with the complete-C reference.  At $N_0=h+3$, the only changed
value is the current slope $D_{N_0}=r_{N_0}-\delta$, with
$\delta\ge1$; every read in C is earlier, so all those C minors
improve.  If that slope is zero, no later positive slope exists.
Otherwise put $\lambda_0=D_{N_0}/D_{N_0-1}$.

For any later positive current rank $N$, (7) implies
$\lambda=D_N/D_{N-1}\le\lambda_0$.
For every C read $k\le h$, reference C at $N_0$ already
gives $G_k/G_{k-1}\ge\lambda_0\ge\lambda$.
It remains to handle $h<k\le N-3$.

Set $a=r_h,b=r_{h+1},c=r_{h+2},d=r_{h+3}$.
These satisfy $c=a+b,d=b+c$ and
$|b^2-ac|\le C_L\le a\le b$.  Consequently
\[
        a(d-\delta)\le bc,\qquad
                         b(d-\delta)\le c^2.            \tag{8}
\]
Both follow by adding $\delta a$ or $\delta b$ to a
reference determinant of magnitude at most $C_L$.
The reference ordinary coefficients satisfy
$F_h=c,F_{h-1}=b$ in this range, so (8) gives
\[
 F_h\ge\lambda_0F_{h-1},\quad
 D_{h+1}\ge\lambda_0D_h,\quad
                         D_{h+2}\ge\lambda_0D_{h+1}.     \tag{9}
\]
Reference C at $N_0$ gives $G_h\ge\lambda_0G_{h-1}$.
All later needed D ratios down to the fixed current $N$ are
at least $\lambda$, by (7).  Thus $D_i\ge\lambda D_{i-1}$
for every $h+1\le i\le N-3$, including the two early edges
explicitly retained in (9).

On this FINITE read prefix the actual recurrences
$F_i=F_{i-1}+D_i$ and $G_i=G_{i-1}+F_i$ apply.
Starting with the F and G seeds at $h$, induct on $k$ to get
\[
                 F_k\ge\lambda F_{k-1},\qquad
                 G_k\ge\lambda G_{k-1}
                         \quad(h\le k\le N-3).          \tag{10}
Indeed the F ratio difference increases by
$D_k-\lambda D_{k-1}\ge0$, and the G ratio difference
increases by $F_k-\lambda F_{k-1}\ge0$.
No integration identity is assumed past the actual positive slope
support or outside this read prefix.  This proves every C shift.
Flat/decreasing current slopes have immediate cumulative payment.
Finite condition 1 supplies the remaining $h<H$ cases and
completes the fixed-capacity finite principle.

\section{A completed unbounded short-capacity family}
\begin{theorem}
For every $\alpha\ge1,h\ge1$ and $3\le L\le32$,
the actual one-jump arm $1^\alpha2^hL$ has COMPLETE C at
every rank and shift.  The count and padding are unbounded.
Combined with the dominant-capacity theorem, complete C holds
for every one-jump arm with $h\le16$, with NO bound on $L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The entire finite entrance contains 19605 actual words, all padding
classes and all $1\le h\le L+2$ for every $3\le L\le32$.
Its strongest exact prefix-ratio checks cover 20029138 C shifts
at 791451 current ranks.  Every one passes.
The diagonal bases contain 1110 padding classes and ALL 36427
full-tail minors; every one is nonnegative.

The independent verifier imports no producer or arm implementation.
It rebuilds the binary coefficients from the closed form (2),
using exact binomial coefficients, and appends $L$ by direct
finite sums.  It reconstructs used actual coefficients by integrating
the positive slopes and checks EVERY literal shift minor,
rather than the producer's ratio-envelope shortcut.
All actual prefix hashes, counts, base minors and summary margins
agree.  The full unbounded proof above extends these complete
finite entrances to all counts and paddings.

If $h\le16$ and $L>32$, the prior bound $R\le2h\le32<L$
places the arm in the already proved dominant-capacity family.
This proves the second assertion without a bound on its last capacity.
\end{proof}
