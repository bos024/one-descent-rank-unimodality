\section{Complete property C for every binary prefix with one arbitrary jump}
Let $\alpha,h\ge1$, $L\ge3$, and let $Y=1^\alpha2^hL$ be
an actual normalized sorted arm.  For an actual arm $Z$, write
\[
 F_Z(n)=[z^n]F_Z(z),\quad
 D_Z(n)=(F_Z(n)-F_Z(n-1))^+\ (n\ge2),\quad D_Z(0)=D_Z(1)=0,
 \quad G_Z(k)=\sum_{i=0}^kF_Z(i).
\]
Negative indices are zero-extended.  The theorem concerns the actual
finite arm, with all its global modes and signed boundary slopes retained.

\begin{theorem}[General one-jump C, all parameters unbounded]
For EVERY $\alpha,h\ge1$, $L\ge3$, every positive-slope current
rank $N$, and every shift $s\ge3$,
\[
           G_Y(N-s)D_Y(N-1)\ge G_Y(N-s-1)D_Y(N).        \tag{1}
\]
There is no bound on padding, binary count, capacity, rank or shift.
In particular the earlier capacity32 cutoff is entirely removed.
\end{theorem}

We use the proved actual ordinary and positive-slope unimodality,
strict ordinary positive-slope prefix starting at two, the only possible
pre-FIRST-D-mode flat at final capacity plus one, and the prior complete-C
one-jump Fibonacci reference.  Their full generation proofs are existing
inputs.  Every new induction below is unbounded.

\subsection*{A modified LC inequality for the ordinary binary tail}
Put $F_h=F_{1^\alpha2^h}$ and
$f_n=\operatorname{Fib}(n+1)$.
Recall the proved entire ordinary binary tail LC at centres $n\ge h+1$,
exact prefix $F_h(n)=f_n$ through $h+1$, and first defect at least one.

\begin{lemma}[Modified binary tail]
If $h\ge2$, $n\ge h+2$ and $F_h(n)>F_h(n-1)$, then
\[
                (F_h(n)-1)^2\ge F_h(n-1)(F_h(n+1)-1).  \tag{2}
\]
Consequently its ordinary minor at such a centre is at least
$2F_h(n)-F_h(n-1)-1\ge F_h(n-1)+1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The exact identity
\[
 H_2-F_h=(z+z^2)^h(H_2-[\alpha+1]),\qquad
                            H_2=(1-z-z^2)^{-1},
\]
gives the first two defects at ranks $h+2,h+3$ as
$(1,h+2)$ for $\alpha\ge3$, $(1,h+3)$ for $\alpha=2$,
and $(2,2h+3)$ for $\alpha=1$.
Put $y=\operatorname{Fib}(h+2)$, $x=\operatorname{Fib}(h+3)$,
$\varepsilon=(-1)^{h+2}$.  The modified minor (2) at $h+2$
therefore equals respectively
\[
 \varepsilon-4x+4+(h+3)y,\quad
 \varepsilon-4x+4+(h+4)y,\quad
 \varepsilon-6x+9+(2h+4)y.                              \tag{3}
\]
These are nonnegative for every $h\ge2$: at $h=2,3,4$ use
the exact Fibonacci values, and at $h\ge5$ use
$x\le2y$, $y\ge0$, $\varepsilon\ge-1$.
These small scalar boundary calculations are kernel checked.

Induct on $h\ge2$.  At $h=2$, its first ordinary mode is at
most four, so only the growing tail centre four can arise; (3) pays it.
For the next count, its first required centre is again paid by (3).
At every later centre write $A,B,C,D$ for four old consecutive values.
The new triple is $(A+B,B+C,C+D)$ and new growth means $C>A$.
If $C>B$, old ordinary LC forces $B>A$; both old modified
inequalities apply.  The new modified minor equals
\[
 \{(B-1)^2-A(C-1)\}+\{(C-1)^2-B(D-1)\}+(BC-AD)-1.     \tag{4}
\]
The integer cross minor is at least one.  Indeed $A\ge1$ by
actual interval support, both old growing ordinary minors are positive,
and $B(BC-AD)=C(B^2-AC)+A(C^2-BD)>0$.
Thus (4) is nonnegative.
If instead $C\le B$, actual ordinary strict-prefix shape puts
$D\le C$.  Integer growth gives $C\ge A+1$, so $B+C-1$
is at least both $A+B$ and $C+D-1$.  Its square dominates
their product, proving (2) in this branch as well.
The complete unbounded family induction is kernel checked,
with the actual support/recurrence/strict-prefix inputs identified here.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{A generic local-valley removal theorem}
\begin{lemma}[Relative-LC transfer at a local valley]
Suppose an incoming actual/reference density triple has values
$(x,xu,z)$ with $0\le u\le1$, $z\le xu^2$, and $x\le1$.
Suppose the outgoing/reference density triple is $(p,q,w)$ with
$0\le q\le p<1$, $0\le w<1$, $q\le w$, and $p\le x$.
Then subtracting the outgoing triple from both sequences preserves
the local relative-LC comparison:
\[
 (xu-q)^2(1-p)(1-w)\ge(x-p)(z-w)(1-q)^2.              \tag{5}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to replace $z$ by $xu^2$.  Let the resulting difference
between the two sides be $P(x)$.  Its quadratic coefficient is
$K=u^2\{(1-p)(1-w)-(1-q)^2\}\le0$.
Thus $P$ is concave on $[p,1]$.  At $p$ its negative product
vanishes, so $P(p)\ge0$.  At one, direct factorization gives
\[
 P(1)=(1-p)(1-u)\{(1-u)c_0+uc_1\},
 \quad c_0=q^2(1-w)+w(1-q)^2,\quad c_1=2(1-q)(w-q).
\]
Both $c_0,c_1$ are nonnegative.  Hence $P(1)\ge0$ and
concavity proves (5).  The kernel proof checks the exact chord identity
and every sign, over arbitrary real parameters.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Uniform analytic entrance for every capacity}
Fix $L\ge3$ and set $H=L+2$.
The reference slopes are
\[
 r_n=\operatorname{Fib}(n)-\operatorname{Fib}(n-L),\quad n\ge2,
 \qquad C_L=\operatorname{Fib}(L-1)+\operatorname{Fib}(L+1)
                                      -1-(-1)^L.        \tag{6}
\]
The prior theorem proves their complete C, with normalized $r_2=1$.
Let $\ell$ be the FIRST positive-D mode of $Y$ and let $R$ be
the first ordinary mode of $F_h$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform entrance density]
For every $1\le h\le L+2$ and every padding $\alpha$, the
TRUNCATED actual density sequence $(D_Y(n)/r_n)_{2\le n\le\ell}$
is log-concave.  In particular complete C holds for every such arm.
No assertion about relative-LC centres beyond this truncated prefix
is needed or made.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The exact signed append slope is
\[
             \Delta F_Y(n)=F_h(n-1)-F_h(n-L-1)\quad(n\ge2). \tag{7}
\]
The established actual mode bounds give $\ell\le R+1\le2h+1$.
For completeness, if $L<R$, the new signed slopes strictly fall
on the edge into $R+2$: its old entering slope is nonpositive
and its old outgoing slope at $R-L+1$ is positive.
Actual positive-D unimodality gives $\ell\le R+1$.
If $L\ge R$, the proved dominant-capacity argument gives the same bound.

If $h\le L+1$, every relative-LC centre $n<\ell$ has
$n-L\le2h-L\le h+1$, so the ENTIRE outgoing triple in (7)
is in the exact binary Fibonacci prefix.
At the diagonal $h=L+2\ge5$, improve the bound to $\ell\le2h$.
Indeed the old signed binary slope at $2h$ is
$1-\binom h{h-\alpha-1}\le1$ (out-of-range binomials are zero),
while the slope at $h+2$ is $\operatorname{Fib}(h+1)-\delta_1>1$,
where $\delta_1=1$ or $2$ is its first defect.
Thus new signed slopes strictly fall on the edge into $2h+1$.
Positive-slope interval support and unimodality force $\ell\le2h$.
Now $n-L\le2h-1-L=h+1$, again covering the WHOLE outgoing triple.

Through $n\le h+2$ the earlier/current actual slopes equal
the reference and the next is no greater.  Their relative minors
are therefore nonnegative.  At every later centre $n<\ell$,
the incoming F index $i=n-1\ge h+2$ is strictly before $R$.
The proved ordinary binary density $F_h(i)/\operatorname{Fib}(i+1)$
is nonincreasing and relatively LC on this whole read prefix.
Also the modified inequality (2) applies.

Put $j=n-L$.  Before the first outgoing value its normalized density
triple is zero, possibly with only a positive next value; it is a local
valley and (5) applies.  For positive even $j$, the outgoing/incoming
Fibonacci density is also a local valley, by the determinant identity
\[
 \operatorname{Fib}(j-1)\operatorname{Fib}(n)
  -\operatorname{Fib}(n-1)\operatorname{Fib}(j)
                       =(-1)^j\operatorname{Fib}(L)>0.
\]
Its next/current comparison has the same sign.  In (5), $x$ is
the previous incoming density, $u$ its next ratio, and $z$ the next
density; all required bounds follow from that normalized relative LC
and from the positive previous actual slope in (7).

For odd $j\ge3$, write $(a,A,C)$ for the incoming actual triple
and $(b,B,d)$ for the outgoing Fibonacci triple.
Then $b\ge1$, $a>b$, $A>a$, $B^2-bd=1$, and (2) gives
$m_A=A^2-aC\ge a+1$.  The exact identity
\[
 ab\{(A-B)^2-(a-b)(C-d)\}
              =(a-b)(b m_A-a)+(bA-aB)^2\ge0            \tag{8}
\]
proves ordinary LC of the actual difference.
At the first outgoing offset $j=1$, the outgoing triple is
$(0,1,1)$, and that ordinary-LC assertion is EXACTLY (2).
For every odd $j\ge1$, the reference difference minor at $n=L+j$
is $-C_L\le0$.  Hence ordinary LC of the positive actual difference
implies relative LC to that reference: in the relative minor, the
actual ordinary minor has a positive reference factor and the
reference ordinary minor is subtracted with a nonnegative actual factor.
This exhausts every centre of the truncated prefix.

Its first two normalized values are one.  The already proved
normalized-relative-C inheritance therefore proves every C comparison
through $\ell$.  Later flat/decreasing positive slopes are immediate
from cumulative monotonicity.  This proves complete C of the uniform
entrance, for ALL capacities and padding lengths.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Propagation needs only LC through the FIRST D mode}
For fixed $L$, put $d_h(j)=D_{1^\alpha2^hL}(j+2)$ and
$a_j=r_{j+2}$, and let $e_h=\ell_{h,L}-2$ be its first D mode
in these shifted indices.  The exact signed recurrence is
\[
       \widetilde d_{h+1}=(z+z^2)\widetilde d_h+1+z-z^{L-1}. \tag{9}
\]
For $h\ge H=L+2$, its fixed forcing is strictly below all tail
reads.  Positive-D interval support implies the exact two-term window
through the penultimate possible support point, with only a possible
reduction/deletion at its final point.  Every next tail coefficient is
therefore at most the corresponding old two-term window.
Also $d_h(j)=a_j$ through $j\le h$ and
$d_h(h+1)\le a_{h+1}-1$.

\begin{lemma}[Whole rising-tail induction]
For every $h\ge H$ and every centre $h\le j\le e_h$, $d_h$
is ordinarily log-concave at $j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At the analytic base $h=H$, the preceding relative-LC proof gives
ordinary LC at every required centre past $h$: even parity has a
nonnegative reference minor and odd parity was paid ordinarily.
At centre $j=h$, the exact first-defect budget gives it directly:
the reference minor has magnitude $C_L$ and its previous value
$r_{h+1}$ is at least $C_L$.
Indeed $r_{L+2}-C_L=\operatorname{Fib}(L-2)+(-1)^L\ge0$,
and the reference increases thereafter.
At the first D mode itself ordinary LC is automatic by maximality.

Induct on $h\ge H$.  At the new first required centre use the
same exact-prefix/first-defect budget.  Consider any later centre
strictly before the new FIRST D mode.  Its next coefficient is
STRICTLY larger: the only possible actual pre-mode flat is at
physical rank $L+1$, whereas these reads have rank at least $H+2$.
The previous/current coefficients equal the old two-term window;
the next is no larger than that window.  Strict next growth forces
$d_h(j)>d_h(j-2)$.  Old positive-D unimodality then puts BOTH
old centres $j-2,j-1$ at or before its FIRST D mode, and they
are in its already proved tail domain.
The sum of their nonnegative LC minors and nonnegative cross minor
proves the window minor.  Reducing the next coefficient can only
improve it.  At the new mode use maximality again.
The full induction is kernel checked; no LC after the old D mode
is assumed.  This is the key strengthening of the earlier
fixed-capacity finite principle.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the general one-jump theorem]
All $h\le H$ are covered by the uniform analytic entrance.
For $h>H$, use the preceding rising-tail LC and the existing
fixed-capacity C assembly.  That assembly reads LC ratios only
through a currently increasing slope, hence only through the FIRST
D mode; its stronger earlier whole-tail premise is unnecessary.
Here are its retained seeds explicitly.

Through $N\le h+2$ the reference and actual slopes/cumulatives agree.
At $N_0=h+3$, only the current slope is reduced by an integer
$\delta\ge1$, so every C minor improves.  If a later current
positive slope increases, let $\lambda=D_N/D_{N-1}$ and
$\lambda_0=D_{N_0}/D_{N_0-1}$.  Rising-tail LC gives
$\lambda\le\lambda_0$.
Put $a=r_h,b=r_{h+1},c=r_{h+2},d=r_{h+3}$.
Since $c=a+b,d=b+c$, $|b^2-ac|\le C_L\le a\le b$,
\[
       a(d-\delta)\le bc,\qquad b(d-\delta)\le c^2.     \tag{10}
\]
These are the TWO early D-ratio seeds; the second also gives the
ordinary F seed at rank $h$, because $F(h)=c,F(h-1)=b$.
Reference C gives the G seed at $h$ and pays every earlier read.
For $h<k\le N-3$, all D ratios read are at least $\lambda$,
including the two explicitly retained in (10).
The exact actual recurrences on this FINITE positive-slope read prefix
propagate $F(k)\ge\lambda F(k-1)$ and then
$G(k)\ge\lambda G(k-1)$.  Thus every C shift is paid.
This finite-read propagation and both scalar seed inequalities are
already kernel checked.  Current flat/decreasing slopes have immediate
payment.  This proves (1) for all parameters, with no finite host
enumeration, capacity cutoff or asymptotic extrapolation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[All actual first-weight-four arms with a capacity at least four]
Every actual sorted arm with first missing weight four and maximum
capacity at least four has complete C.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
With at most two non-one capacities use the established general C
theorem through five.  With at least three, deleting its last capacity
leaves first weight three and at least two non-one capacities, hence
an entirely binary prefix.  It is exactly a one-jump arm and (1) applies.
\end{proof}
