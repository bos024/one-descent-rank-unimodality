\section{A sharp first-missing-five source-cone criterion and a larger original family}
This is a refinement of the frozen
\texttt{binary\_ternary\_A2\_original\_family\_proof.tex}.
It reuses its ten scoped kernels and adds NO new formal-report count.

\begin{theorem}[The first-missing-five criterion]
Let $\mathcal K$ be a final-deletion-closed class of normalized actual
increasing arms, including its rectangular and empty bases. Assume
EVERY nonrectangular member has complete C and first missing weight
$\kappa\le5$. Then every pair in $\mathcal K$ has full A2.
For two nonrectangular members the ENTIRE raw source
$E=(2-z)q_Xq_Y$ has unimodal positive part with initial positive support.
For all strict central peaks, the ORIGINAL core and BOTH relative
polynomials are coefficient unimodal. All these A2 and original
coefficient conclusions also hold after ONE arbitrary legal final
append to either arm, provided that new arm is heavier.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All steps of the first-missing-four criterion apply unchanged except
for the mixed source comparison when the rising opposite has
$\kappa=5$. If the nonincreasing owner has at least two non-one
parts, the opposite has at least three, because $\kappa\le h+2$.
The existing sharp gap-five theorem in
\texttt{source\_birth\_gap\_nonrecovery.tex} therefore applies:
$\alpha\ge1\ge\kappa-5$, and BOTH required count hypotheses hold.
It proves the needed source nonrecovery at every such mixed rank.
Its complete finite complement and independent verifier were proved
earlier and are reused, not relabelled as new numerical evidence.

If the owner has ONE non-one part, write it as $Z=1^\alpha s$,
$s\ge2$. Its exact reverse polynomial and positive differences are
\[
 q_Z=[\alpha+1][s]+z^{a},\quad a=\alpha+s,\qquad
 d_r=\mathbf1_{0\le r<\min(\alpha+1,s)}.       \tag{1}
\]
The terminal unit creates no positive increment because the preceding
coefficient is also one. The positive primitive in (1) has NO strictly
increasing edge. In the mixed identity (1) of the preceding proof,
$r\ge0$ could have positive curvature only at such an edge, so it
cannot recover. For $r\le-2$ there is no curvature. For $r=-1$ the
curvature is one and the compulsory opposite source pays it at
$a+2-5=a-3\in[0,a-1]$. Rank ZERO is retained here: for the smallest
owner $1,2$, the payment is $A_0=1$. Thus the mixed resource is
complete also for one-part owners.

Consequently unconditional A1 and the correctly guarded post-LAST-mode
resource (Q) hold for every nonrectangular $\mathcal K$ pair.
Full old A2, the entire maximum plateau, (Q), and A1 give the same
whole signed source cone with no holes. The old signed-window theorem
then gives NEW A2 before the heavier new first mode; rectangles and
heavier-one-part bases close the induction. The actual full A2,
proper-relative signed prefix, lower-peak base and original joins are
exactly the previously proved assemblies.

For the heavier-append original family, deletion preserves the
qualifying heavier arm or removes its new jump. When stripping a
largest capacity, if it is the qualifying jump remove it first;
otherwise it lies only in the opposite arm, so removal preserves
heaviness. The lower-peak induction therefore stays in the family.
No C property or small first missing weight is assumed of the NEW
heavier appended arm.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Explicit unbounded classes with all inputs already proved}
Define $\mathcal A_5$ to be the union of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item ALL $1^\alpha2^u3^r$;
\item ALL $1^\alpha2^uV$ with at most TWO arbitrary nonbinary
parts in the sorted tail $V$ (each at least three);
\item ALL $1^\alpha2^u3^rT$, $r\ge1$,
$T\ge6r$.
\end{enumerate}
Here $\alpha\ge1$, all binary/ternary counts and all stated capacities
are unbounded; include rectangular and empty bases. Complete C for
the first class is the new binary/ternary theorem, for the second
the complete two-nonbinary theorem, and for the third the uniform
dominant-append theorem of
\texttt{relative\_golden\_head\_dominant\_C\_proof.tex}, using old
nonbinary weight $3r$ and $T\ge2(3r)$. These are complete C at every
rank and shift, including arbitrary binary count and finite padding.
Every nonrectangular member has $\kappa\le5$, by the exact first
missing recurrence; the sorted tail has at most two sources that can
raise the binary value three, or is a ternary arm of first missing
weight at most four followed by one jump. Deleting a last part stays
in the union. Thus the theorem proves full A2 and ORIGINAL core/BOTH
relative coefficient unimodality for ANY two members of $\mathcal A_5$,
at every strict central peak. It also permits ONE arbitrary heavier
append from this class. There is no weight-order restriction for the
unappended $\mathcal A_5$ pairs.

\subsection{The first-missing bound is sharp for this whole-source method}
Take the actual arms
\[
                    X=(1,2),\qquad Y=(1,5,5,5,5).
\]
They have $\kappa_X=3$, $\kappa_Y=6$, weights three and twenty-one,
and BOTH have complete C by the established weight-at-most-thirty-two
theorem. In forward order put $P=F_XF_Y$. Exact extraction from
$F_X=1+z+2z^2+z^3$ and
$F_Y=\sum_{i=0}^3S_5^i+S_5^4(1+z)$ gives
\[
             (P_3,P_4,P_5,P_6)=(9,17,34,65).
\]
For the reverse product $p_n=P_{24-n}$ its ENTIRE raw source has
\[
                 (E_{19},E_{20},E_{21})=(3,0,1).       \tag{2}
\]
Therefore $E^+$ is neither unimodal nor supported on an initial
interval. This disproves replacing five by six in the UNIVERSAL
whole-source-cone conclusion, even when BOTH arms have full C.
It does NOT disprove full A2, original coefficient unimodality, or
any of the correctly scoped source estimates. The new closure
criterion is sharp for its stated raw-source conclusion; it is not
asserted to be a sharp boundary for the original conjecture.
