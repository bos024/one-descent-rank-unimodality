\section{A triangular lower bound for source curvature through the first derivative mode}
\paragraph{Verification status.}
The unbounded written induction below is complete, using two independently
rebuilt exact finite coefficient certificates. Twenty-one scoped Lean reports
check the generic payments and every one of the 53 exceptional finite rows.
The actual-arm identifications and complete finite-grammar closure remain
the written assembly and independently checked programs; no end-to-end
Lean formalization is asserted. This is a SINGLE-arm theorem and does not
solve the original conjecture.

For an actual normalized nonrectangular sorted arm put
\[
 E=1-z+(2z-1)F,\quad \kappa=\min\{i:E_i>0\},
 \quad\delta=E_\kappa,\quad B(d)=\max\{1,\tbinom{d+1}{2}-1\}.
\]
Set $D_0=D_1=0$, $D_i=(F_i-F_{i-1})^+$ for $i\ge2$, and
$\ell=\min\operatorname{argmax}D$. All extensions are zero outside
the coefficient support.

\begin{theorem}[Triangular source-curvature target]
For every such arm,
\[
                      \Delta E_n\ge B(\delta)
                             \qquad(\kappa+3\le n\le\ell). \tag{TC}
\]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Established actual inputs.}
The ordinary $F$ and the positive derivative $D$ are unimodal;
$D$ has positive interval support $[2,R]$, where $R$ is the first
ordinary mode of $F$. In particular $D_2=1$.
Actual derivative doubling gives $D_i\le2D_{i-1}$ for $i\ge3$.
Consequently $\Delta E_i\ge0$ through rank $R$; for $3\le i\le R$
the exact formula is $\Delta E_i=2D_{i-1}-D_i$.
Legal sorted appends increase every positive derivative coefficient.
We also use the proved first-missing source profile and early scaled
dyadic bounds, and the exact append splice
\[
 \widehat D=P\ \hbox{through the last old ordinary mode plus one},
 \quad P=z^2[t-1]+S_tD,
\]
after which $\widehat D$ is nonincreasing. These are prior actual-arm
theorems, not scalar assumptions inferred from numerical tests.

\begin{lemma}[The two mode bounds and an early-peak rank budget]
For every legal append,
\[
                  \widehat\ell\le\min(R+1,\ell+t).       \tag{1}
\]
If $n\le\widehat\ell$ and $n-1>\ell$, writing $M=D_\ell$ gives
\[
                 \Delta\widehat E_n\ge M+n-\ell-3.       \tag{2}
\]
If additionally $\kappa\le7$ and $\ell\le\kappa+2$, every failure
of (TC) after this append has $n\le45$ and $M\le128$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After rank $R+1$, the entering derivative in $S_tD$ is zero, so
the window and the additional interval are nonincreasing there.
After rank $\ell+t$, both entering and leaving indices are on the
nonincreasing tail of $D$, and the additional interval has finished.
Thus $P$ has first mode at most both displayed bounds; the exact
splice and its nonincreasing tail give the same result for $\widehat D$.

Use the exact source identity
\[
 \Delta\widehat E_n=\sum_{i=n-t}^{n-1}\Delta E_i
              +\mathbf1_{n=t+1}-\mathbf1_{n=t+2}.       \tag{3}
\]
The window contains $\ell+1$, since $n-t\le\ell$ and
$n-1>\ell$. Its summand is at least $M$.
Each of the $n-\ell-2$ subsequent summands through $n-1$ is
at least one: by (1) these indices are at most $R$, and
$2D_{i-1}-D_i\ge D_{i-1}\ge1$ on this descending positive tail.
Every remaining summand is nonnegative. Pay the possible negative
unit in (3), proving (2).

For the last assertion, $\widehat\kappa\le\kappa+1\le8$ and
$\widehat\delta\le8$, so $B(\widehat\delta)\le35$.
The first old mode is at most nine. Since $M\ge1$, a failure
and (2) give $n\le35+9+2-1=45$.
Doubling from $D_2=1$ gives $M\le2^{\ell-2}\le128$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Every late old peak is paid using OLD TC]
Assume OLD TC, and suppose $\ell\ge\kappa+3$.
Then every NEW comparison with $n\le\widehat\ell$ and $n-1>\ell$
satisfies (TC), at every first missing weight.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both $\ell$ and $\ell+1$ are in the window (3). The first term
is at least $B(\delta)$ by OLD TC, and the second at least $M$.
All remaining terms are nonnegative. Thus the payment is at least
$B(\delta)+M-1$, or $B(\delta)+M$ when the negative unit is absent.
Stationary appends reset the new multiplicity to one, and preserving
growth keeps it equal to $\delta$; these are paid because $M\ge1$.

For tied growth $t=\kappa$, the new multiplicity is $\delta+1$.
The negative unit occurs at $n=\kappa+2$, before the required
new range $n\ge\kappa+4$. We show $M\ge\delta+1$.
The universal old prefix gives
\[
       D_{\kappa-1}=2^{\kappa-3}=:p,
              \qquad D_\kappa=2p-\delta.
\]
Both indices are strictly before the FIRST mode $\ell$, so
$D_\kappa\ge D_{\kappa-1}$ and $M>D_{\kappa-1}$.
Therefore $\delta\le p<M$, hence $M\ge\delta+1$ by integrality.
This pays $B(\delta+1)-B(\delta)$, which is at most $\delta+1$.
No low-first-weight classification is needed.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A complete finite jet grammar.}
Fix a prefix endpoint $N$. The initial all-ones prefixes are represented
exactly by $1\le\alpha\le N$: larger initial runs agree with $\alpha=N$.
Clamping every capacity and its legal append at $N$ preserves all forward
coefficients through rank $N$, preserves sorted order, and preserves each
first missing weight at most $N$. A state consists of this exact coefficient
tuple, its clamped last capacity, and its first missing weight. Its leading
multiplicity is determined by the coefficient tuple, so no extra hidden
state is needed. The transition is exactly
\[
            F'_0=1,\qquad F'_n=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(t,n)}F_{n-j}.
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
Equal states have equal transitions under every future legal append.
Starting from all the initial states and closing (4) under all legal
clamped capacities therefore contains EVERY actual prefix in the stated
first-weight range, by induction on the word length. First missing weights
never decrease, so states above a retained first-weight cap may be discarded.
The complete queue must be exhausted; a time or state-limited run is not
a certificate. No global mode is claimed to survive clipping. The finite
calculations below use only coefficient identities and conditions that
are NECESSARY for the unbounded actual modes in each proved case.

\paragraph{Completion by induction.}
Induct on the number of non-one parts. All-ones arms and one-non-one
arms have no required TC comparison: the latter have first positive
derivative mode two. Otherwise delete the final legal capacity and use
OLD TC by induction. The case $n-1\le\ell$ is closed by the generic
pre-old-peak proof and the complete endpoint-$13$ boundary calculation.
For $n-1>\ell$, old $\kappa\ge8$ is covered by the prior unconditional
peak-crossing mass theorem. At old $\kappa\le7$, late old peaks are
covered by the OLD-TC lemma above, and early old peaks by the rank
reduction and endpoint-$45$ calculation. These cases exhaust the
induction. No parameter outside the verified finite jets is silently
bounded.

Even after final verification of this assembly, the theorem supplies
a one-arm source-curvature resource. The entire later two-source
overlap convolution, the residual c0 mixed head, general A2 and the
original coefficient-unimodality conjecture remain separate problems.
As an immediate corollary $2\Delta E_n\ge\delta^2$ on the same range:
for $\delta\ge2$, $2B(\delta)=\delta^2+\delta-2\ge\delta^2$,
and $\delta=1$ is immediate. Thus the FIRST two-source overlap has
the short payment $B(d)+B(e)\ge de$, by the nonnegative square
$(d-e)^2$. This supplies a second proof of the already completed
first-overlap halving branch. It does not pay the growing convolution
at arbitrary later overlaps.
The prior statement that the triangular bound was unproved is superseded
by this theorem; the stronger pointwise bound by the two actual leading
source coefficients remains false, as its earlier counterexample shows.
