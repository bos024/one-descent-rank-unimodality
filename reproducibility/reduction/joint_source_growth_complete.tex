\section{Two leading source coefficients and a growing offset budget}
This section proves two SINGLE sorted-arm theorems. The actual-arm append
assembly and the finite clipping argument are written proofs; the scoped
Lean kernels check the complete pre-peak window payments and scalar
reductions, and a separate kernel certificate checks the entire 106-state
low-offset closure. The larger peak certificate is rebuilt independently
with exact equality of every state. No end-to-end Lean formalization of
actual composition ideals or of the large enumeration is asserted.

Use the normalized actual sorted arm $1^\alpha U$, with $\alpha\ge1$,
and put
\[
 E=1-z+(2z-1)F,\quad \kappa=\min\{i:E_i>0\},\quad
 \delta=E_\kappa,\quad\eta=E_{\kappa+1},\quad J=\delta+\eta.
\]
Write $D_0=D_1=0$, $D_i=(F_i-F_{i-1})^+$ for $i\ge2$,
$\ell=\min\operatorname{argmax}D$ and $M=D_\ell$. Nonrectangular
arms have $\kappa\ge3$. The early source profile gives $\eta\ge1$.

\begin{lemma}[The second offset is positive through the first mode]
For every actual nonrectangular sorted arm,
\[
            \ell\ge\kappa+2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
                    \Delta E_{\kappa+2}\ge1.                 \tag{1}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\kappa\ge5$, the genuine early source-curvature theorem in
\texttt{source\_birth\_gap\_refinements.tex} gives
$\Delta E_{\kappa+L}\ge2^{L-2}$ for $2\le L\le\kappa-3$.
This is a theorem about increments, not a differentiated coefficient bound.
Take $L=2$.

For $\kappa=3,4$, retain coefficients $F_0,\ldots,F_6$, the last
capacity clamped to six, and $\kappa$. Clamp $\alpha$ and all capacities
to six. This preserves these seven coefficients, sortedness, and every
transition with first missing weight at most four. The exact recurrence is
$F'=1+S_tF$, and $\kappa'=\min(\kappa+1,t+1)$.
Since $\kappa$ never decreases, all ancestors of a final $\kappa\le4$
remain in this grammar. The entire grammar consists of 106 states and
179 retained edges, starting at the six initial all-ones prefixes.
\texttt{SecondOffsetSourceCertificate.lean} checks ALL base states,
ALL retained edges and closure, and ALL curvature alternatives by
kernel reduction. In each of the 15 states where the curvature in (1)
is smaller than one, a strict decrease $D_i<D_{i-1}$ occurs for
$3\le i\le\kappa+2$. Unimodality of $D$ therefore forces
$\ell<i\le\kappa+2$, contradicting the hypothesis. The exact same
106-state set is also the projection of the independently reconstructed
complete 13-jet certificate. Induction on actual appended words proves
coverage; no global mode is asserted to survive clamping.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Joint leading-source curvature]
For every actual nonrectangular sorted arm,
\[
             \boxed{\Delta E_n\ge\delta+\eta
                         \quad(\kappa+4\le n\le\ell).}       \tag{J}
\]
The starting offset cannot be reduced to three: for
$X=1^{44}2^{10}$, $\kappa=3$, $\delta=\eta=1$, and
$\Delta E_6=1<2$, with $6\le\ell$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct strongly on the number of non-one capacities. An all-ones arm
followed by one capacity has first derivative mode two, so the claimed
range is empty. For the induction step the OLD arm is nonrectangular.
Use hats for the NEW arm after a legal append $t\ge\kappa-1$.
The exact source window is
\[
 \Delta\widehat E_n
   =\sum_{i=n-t}^{n-1}\Delta E_i
           +\mathbf1_{n=t+1}-\mathbf1_{n=t+2}.                \tag{2}
\]
The three legal source transitions give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 t&\widehat\kappa&\widehat\delta&\widehat\eta\\ \hline
 \kappa-1&\kappa&1&\delta\\
 \kappa&\kappa+1&\delta+1&\eta+\delta\\
 >\kappa&\kappa+1&\delta&\eta+\delta+\mathbf1_{t=\kappa+1}.
\end{array}                                                   \tag{3}
\]
We use the already proved triangular curvature theorem
\[
 \Delta E_i\ge B(\delta):=\max\{1,\tbinom{\delta+1}{2}-1\}
                  \quad(\kappa+3\le i\le\ell).                \tag{4}
\]
In particular $B(\delta)\ge\delta$ and $B(\delta)\ge1$.

First suppose $n-1\le\ell$. Every summand in (2) is nonnegative.
For a stationary append, $\widehat J=\delta+1$. At its first required
rank $n=\kappa+4$, the distinct terms $i=\kappa+3,\kappa+2$
belong to the window and pay $\delta+1$ by (4) and (1).
At every later rank $n\ge\kappa+5$, the term $i=n-1$ pays
$J=\delta+\eta\ge\delta+1$ by OLD (J). Both isolated units lie
before this range.

For either growth append, $n\ge\kappa+5$ and $t\ge\kappa\ge3$.
The three distinct terms $n-1,n-2,n-3$ lie in the window.
OLD (J) pays $J$ at $n-1$, and (4) pays $\delta$ at $n-2$.
At $n-3$, (1) pays one when $n=\kappa+5$, and (4) pays at least
one at every later rank. Thus the window pays $J+\delta+1$.
For tied growth, this is exactly $\widehat J$ and both units have
already finished. For preserving growth, it exceeds $\widehat J$
by one unless $t=\kappa+1$. In that exceptional case the negative
unit is at $n=\kappa+3$, outside the required range. At every
negative-unit rank that is in the required range, $t\ge\kappa+3$,
so the spare unit pays it. This proves every pre-old-peak comparison.
The complete finite-window payment, including all these cases, is
the theorem \texttt{joint\_pre\_peak\_window\_payment} in
\texttt{JointSourceGrowth.lean}.

It remains to treat $n-1>\ell$. The actual mode/splice theorem gives
$\widehat\ell\le\min(R+1,\ell+t)$, where $R$ is the first ordinary
mode of $F$. The exact window (2) contains $\ell+1$, whose increment
is at least $M$. Every subsequent summand through $n-1\le R$
is at least one. Every other summand is nonnegative. Consequently
\[
 \Delta\widehat E_n\ge M-1,\qquad
 \Delta\widehat E_n\ge M+n-\ell-3\ge n-5.                      \tag{5}
\]
The last inequality uses the established sharp bound $\ell\le M+2$.

For old $\kappa\ge8$, the universal prefix gives
$M\ge D_\kappa=2^{\kappa-2}-\delta\ge2^{\kappa-2}-\kappa$.
The proved first-source jet bounds imply
\[
 \widehat J\le\widehat\kappa+
       \frac{\widehat\kappa(\widehat\kappa+1)}2-1
       \le\frac{(\kappa+1)(\kappa+4)}2-1.                     \tag{6}
\]
For every integer $\kappa\ge8$,
\[
       2\cdot2^{\kappa-2}\ge\kappa^2+7\kappa+4.              \tag{7}
\]
At eight this is $128\ge124$. Doubling the polynomial dominates
its successor since the difference is $\kappa^2+5\kappa-4\ge0$.
This proves (7) by unbounded induction and makes $M-1\ge\widehat J$.

For old $\kappa\le7$, one has $\widehat\kappa\le8$ and
$\widehat J\le43$. By (5), any failure must have
\[
                             M\le43,\qquad n\le47.           \tag{8}
\]
The complete 47-jet certificate below pays every comparison in (8),
closing this last branch and the strong induction.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Complete peak-crossing certificate and coverage.}
Retain $F_0,\ldots,F_{47}$, the last capacity clamped to 47, and
old $\kappa\le7$. Start with the 47 initial all-ones prefixes;
append every legal clamped capacity $2\le t\le47$. A new first
weight eight is evaluated but not queued. Retain a queued state only
when every retained positive derivative coefficient is at most 43.
This pruning loses no potential failure: legal actual appends increase
every positive derivative coefficient, so every ancestor of a failing
old arm with $M\le43$ has this property. Clamping preserves all
read coefficients. Since every retained $D_i\le43$, its coefficients
are at most $1+46\cdot43$, safely represented as unsigned 16-bit
integers. Equal states have equal future coefficients and transitions.

The entire exhausted graph has 207049 states, 3905898 legal appends,
3903736 eligible nonrectangular appends, 3678542 derivative-budget
prunings and 53508829 necessary-guard comparisons, all paid.
The necessary guards are $n\ge\widehat\kappa+4$, $n\ge m+2$,
$n\le m+t$, nondecrease of the NEW derivative through $n$, and
$D_{n-1}>0$, where $m$ is the first mode of the retained OLD
derivative prefix. For a genuine failure (8), $\ell\le M+2\le45$,
so $m=\ell$; every guard is necessary by the mode bounds used in (5).
The retained predicate is a superset of the actual comparisons, not
an assertion that a truncated ordinary polynomial has the same modes.

The independent verifier uses a depth-first traversal and rolling sums,
rather than the producer's direct prefix sums and breadth-first traversal.
It reconstructs every state and comparison, then compares the ENTIRE
sorted raw state stream byte for byte; a hash collision assumption is
not used. Both programs give the counts above and no unpaid comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Offset-linear source curvature]
Every actual nonrectangular sorted arm also satisfies
\[
          \boxed{\Delta E_n\ge n-\kappa-2
                         \quad(\kappa+3\le n\le\ell).}       \tag{L}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Again induct on the number of non-one capacities; the one-capacity
range is empty. A comparison crossing the old first derivative peak
is paid directly by (5): $n-5\ge n-\widehat\kappa-2$ since
$\widehat\kappa\ge3$.
Before that peak all window summands are nonnegative.
For stationary growth $\widehat\kappa=\kappa$, the first rank
$n=\kappa+3$ is paid by the contained second-offset term (1).
At $n=\kappa+4$, the second-offset term and (4) pay two.
At every $n\ge\kappa+5$, OLD (L) at $n-1$ pays $n-\kappa-3$
and (4) at the distinct window index $n-2$ pays the remaining one.
The isolated units lie outside these ranks.
For a growth step $\widehat\kappa=\kappa+1$, OLD (L) at $n-1$
already pays $n-\widehat\kappa-2$. If the negative unit is present,
$n=t+2\ge\kappa+4$, so the additional distinct birth index
$\kappa$ is in the window $[2,t+1]$ and supplies
$\Delta E_\kappa=\delta\ge1$. This pays the unit loss.
The complete pre-peak finite-window argument is checked by
\texttt{linear\_pre\_peak\_window\_payment}.
Thus (L) requires no additional large enumeration beyond (1) and
the already established triangular and single-flat theorems.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A further complete source-overlap subcase.}
At the second overlap of two arms with first weights $p,q\ge3$,
leading multiplicities $\delta_X=\delta_Y=1$, and both positive
forward derivatives strictly increasing, put $j+1=p+q+1$.
Then $j+1\ge p+4,q+4$, and the compared index is at or before
both FIRST derivative modes. By (J),
\[
 \Delta E_X(j+1)+\Delta E_Y(j+1)
    \ge2+\eta_X+\eta_Y\ge (E_XE_Y)_{p+q+1}.
\]
The exact product contrast therefore gives tail halving. The case
where either forward derivative is flat or falling is covered by the
earlier direct nonrecovery theorem. Nonnegative tail and the usual
OLD head premise then give the corresponding all-head S statement.
No general higher-multiplicity second overlap, arbitrary later overlap,
general C, A2, or original unamplified coefficient unimodality is claimed.
