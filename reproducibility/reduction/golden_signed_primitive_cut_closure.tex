\section{A geometric cut closure from the later positive primitive}
NEW proof under formal and independent audit; no registration is
made in this file alone.

Let $0<q<1$ satisfy $q^2+q=1$, and write $\phi=1/q=1+q$.
Let $b$ be positive LC on the finite interval $0,\ldots,H$, with
zero extension. Let $M$ be its FIRST mode and $d_n=b_n-b_{n-1}$,
including $d_{H+1}=-b_H$. Assume $(d_n)^+\mathbf1_{n\ge1}$ is LC.
This permits an exceptional primitive minor at centre one.
For every integer $a\ge0$ define
\[
 x_n=\sum_{j\ge1}q^j b_{n+j},\quad E_n=b_n+qb_{n-1},\quad
                         A_n=(x_n-E_{n-a})^+.
\]
Then $A$ is LC at every centre $a+2\le n<M$.

\begin{proof}
On integer indices put
\[
 K_a(j)=\begin{cases}
 q^{-j},&j\le-1,\\
 q,&0\le j<a,\\
 q^3,&j=a,\\
 0,&j>a.
 \end{cases}
\]
Its nonzero ratios, in order, are $1/q$, then one if that block is
present, then $q^2$. For $a=0$ there is simply the jump from $1/q$
to $q^2$. Thus $K_a$ is LC with interval support. Its infinite left
tail can be truncated below any finite read band; this preserves LC.

The recurrence $x_{n-1}=q(x_n+b_n)$ gives
$\Delta x_n=q^2x_n-qb_n$. Also
\[
 E_{n-a}=\phi b_n-\phi\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}d_{n-k}-qd_{n-a}.
\]
Since $q^2\phi=q$, exact substitution gives
\[
                 q^2(x_n-E_{n-a})=(K_a*d)_n.          \tag{1}
\]
All sums over $d$ are finite. Negative $d$ occur only at indices
$k>M$. For $n\le M$ these indices use only the geometric left tail
of $K_a$. Their contribution is $-T\phi^n$, where
$T=\sum_{k>M}q^k(-d_k)\ge0$. If also $n>a$, the $d_0$ term
vanishes because $K_a$ has upper support endpoint $a$.
Consequently on $a<n\le M$ the right side of (1) equals
\[
 F_n-T\phi^n,\qquad
 F=K_a*((d_n)^+\mathbf1_{n\ge1}).                    \tag{2}
\]
The sequence $F$ is nonnegative LC by ordinary LC convolution;
its finite-read truncations agree with this formula.

Dividing by $\phi^n$ preserves LC. For any nonnegative LC triple
$(l,v,r)$ and constant $T\ge0$, subtracting $T$ and taking positive
parts preserves LC. If both neighbouring positive parts are nonzero,
then $l,r>T$; LC forces $v>T$, and the unclipped determinant is
\[
 (v-T)^2-(l-T)(r-T)
   =v^2-lr+T(l+r-2v)\ge0.
\]
When $l+r\ge2v$ this follows directly from LC. When $l+r\le2v$,
rewrite the same determinant as
$(v-l)^2+(l-T)(2v-l-r)\ge0$. If either neighbouring positive part
is zero the assertion is automatic. The superlevel support is an
interval, because the original sequence is LC. Apply this to (2)
and multiply back by $\phi^n$. Every triple at centre
$a+2\le n<M$ lies entirely in the exact range $a<n\le M$.
\end{proof}

This theorem uses LC of the positive primitive of $b$ ITSELF after
deleting rank zero. LC of the positive primitive of $E$ alone is
insufficient, as the earlier registered exact non-CI counterexample
shows. Nothing in that counterexample contradicts this theorem.

are checked by \texttt{GoldenPrimitiveCut.lean}; the finite convolution,
sign support and geometric-tail assembly are written arguments.
