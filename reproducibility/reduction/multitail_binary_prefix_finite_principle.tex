\section{An all-padding finite principle for every fixed nonbinary tail}
Fix a nonempty sorted word $V=(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$, $t_i\ge3$, and put
\[
 W=\sum_i t_i,\quad h=W-r,\quad H=h+4,\quad
 F_{u,\alpha}=F_{1^\alpha2^u V},\quad
 D_{u,\alpha}(n)=(F_{u,\alpha}(n)-F_{u,\alpha}(n-1))^+\ (n\ge2).
\]
As usual the separate atom at zero is one and the slope at one is zero.
Let $F^\star$ be the reference obtained by appending $V$ to
$1/(1-z-z^2)$, let $r_n=F^\star_n-F^\star_{n-1}$ for $n\ge2$,
and let $G^\star=F^\star/(1-z)$. All assertions below concern actual
coefficient comparisons, not an inference from cumulative log-concavity.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-tail finite principle]
For THIS fixed $V$, the following two finite conditions imply complete
property C of $1^\alpha2^uV$ for EVERY $u\ge H$ and $\alpha\ge1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The reference satisfies all C shifts at all $N\le W+5$.
\item At $u=H$, for every $1\le\alpha\le H+h+1$, its positive slopes
      are ordinarily log-concave at every centre
      $H+r+1\le n<\ell_{H,\alpha}$, where $\ell$ is the FIRST
      positive-slope mode.
\end{enumerate}
The second condition requires only ordinary coefficients through
$2H+W+1$. Without condition 1, condition 2 alone still proves ALL C
shifts at EVERY actual current rank $N\ge W+6$, for all $u\ge H$
and all initial paddings.
If additionally complete actual C is checked for
\[
 0\le u<H,\qquad 1\le\alpha\le u+h+1,
\]
using coefficients through $2u+W+1$, complete C follows for ALL
$u\ge0$ and ALL $\alpha\ge1$. The number, values and weight of the
fixed word $V$ are unrestricted in this implication. No assertion
that its finite entrance always passes is included.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{A word-uniform Cassini budget}
Write
\[
 C_t=\operatorname{Fib}(t-1)+\operatorname{Fib}(t+1)-1-(-1)^t,
 \qquad A_V=\prod_{t\in V} C_t.
\]
The homogeneous reference slope has Cassini minor of absolute value
$A_V$. Indeed, beyond the finite numerator, appending $t$ convolves
the homogeneous Fibonacci sequence with $S_t=z+\cdots+z^t$.
On its two characteristic roots the two multipliers have product
$S_t(\varphi^{-1})S_t(-\varphi)$, whose absolute value is $C_t$.
Equivalently this follows by the $2$ by $2$ determinant of
$\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}^j$.
The empty reference has unit amplitude.

For every $t\ge2$,
\[
       1\le C_t\le\operatorname{Fib}(t+2)-1.             \tag{1}
\]
For $t=2$ this is direct. For $t\ge3$, the difference on the right
is $\operatorname{Fib}(t-2)+(-1)^t\ge0$.
If $a_j$ is any nonnegative homogeneous Fibonacci tail, then
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{t-1}a_{m+j}
 =\{\operatorname{Fib}(t+2)-1\}a_m
  +\{\operatorname{Fib}(t+1)-1\}a_{m-1}
 \ge C_ta_m.                                             \tag{2}
\]
Starting from the binary reference $r_2=1$ and appending the WHOLE
word, (2) proves by induction
\[
            r_{W+2}\ge A_V,\qquad
            r_{W+4}\ge2A_V+1.                            \tag{3}
\]
All reads in this induction are in the old homogeneous tail: at an
append they start at old weight plus two. The reference is positive
from rank two. Thus the final extra unit in (3) is legitimate.

\subsection*{Exact finite padding coverage}
Actual ordinary unimodality gives first ordinary mode at most
$2u+W$: the initial indicator has first mode zero, and appending
capacity $t$ increases this bound by at most $t$, since beyond the
old bound plus $t$ its signed slope reads two old coefficients in
the nonincreasing tail. The positive-slope support, and hence its
first mode, lies no later than $2u+W$.

Changing initial padding from $\alpha$ to $\alpha'>\alpha$ changes
the actual polynomial first at rank $u+r+\alpha+1$, because its
whole difference is $z^{u+r}(1+z)^u\prod_i[t_i]$ times the change
of the initial indicator. Therefore clamping padding to
$u+h+1$ preserves EVERY ordinary coefficient through $2u+W+1$.
This proves full coverage of the finite tests, including the zero
successor of the positive-slope support. It does not assume mode
preservation under arbitrary clipping.

\subsection*{The infinite binary-count propagation}
For every fixed padding the WHOLE polynomial identity is
\[
 F_{u+1,\alpha}=(z+z^2)F_{u,\alpha}+B_V,\qquad
 B_V=(1-z-z^2)F^\star,\quad \deg B_V=W.                  \tag{4}
\]
This inserts a two BEFORE $V$, rather than appending an illegal
two after $V$. Its signed-slope forcing $(1-z)B_V$ has degree
$W+1$. The actual and reference coefficients agree through
$u+r+1$, and at
\[
             N_0=u+r+2
\]
the first ordinary and slope defect is $\delta=2$ if $\alpha=1$,
and $\delta=1$ otherwise.
For $u\ge H$ we have $N_0\ge W+6$. At its preceding centre,
the exact-prefix identity and (3) give
\[
 D_{u,\alpha}(N_0-1)^2
 -D_{u,\alpha}(N_0-2)D_{u,\alpha}(N_0)
 \ge -A_V+\delta r_{N_0-2}\ge A_V+1>0.                 \tag{5}
\]

Induct on $u\ge H$ to prove ordinary LC at all centres
$u+r+1\le n\le\ell_{u,\alpha}$.
The finite condition supplies the base; at the mode itself LC is
automatic by maximality. For the new first centre use (5).
At every later centre STRICTLY before the new first mode, the next
new slope is strictly larger. Here the known unique possible pre-mode
flat, at last capacity plus one, is earlier than $W+5$ and therefore
outside the entire tail being considered.

Above degree $W+1$ the old positive slopes form the exact two-term
window for the new previous and current coefficients; its next is
at most that window. Positive-support interval structure shows that
only the last new positive coefficient could be reduced by a signed
old outgoing value. If the old consecutive quadruple is $(a,b,c,d)$,
strict new next growth forces $d>b$. Old slope unimodality then
puts BOTH old LC centres at or before the old FIRST mode. They also
lie in the inductively certified tail. Consequently
\[
 (b+c)^2-(a+b)(c+d)
  =(b^2-ac)+(c^2-bd)+(bc-ad)\ge0.                       \tag{6}
\]
The cross term follows from the two old LC minors and $b>0$.
Reducing the new next coefficient improves the desired minor.
At the new mode use maximality again. This is precisely the
previously kernel-checked whole rising-reference-tail induction,
under the shifted indexing $d_u(j)=D_{u,\alpha}(j+r+1)$.
No LC after the old first mode is assumed.

\subsection*{Complete C from the propagated ordinary LC}
The previously proved all-word reference theorem supplies ALL C
shifts at every $N\ge W+6$. Exact-prefix agreement therefore pays
all actual ranks $W+6\le N<N_0$; at $N_0$ reducing only the
current slope improves every C minor.

For later strictly increasing current slopes, put
$\lambda=D_N/D_{N-1}$. If their first mode is earlier than $N_0$,
there is nothing left to prove. Otherwise tail LC gives
\[
           \lambda\le\lambda_0=(r_{N_0}-\delta)/r_{N_0-1}.
\]
Write $(a,b,c,d)=(r_{N_0-3},r_{N_0-2},r_{N_0-1},r_{N_0})$.
They are homogeneous, $c=a+b$, $d=b+c$, and (3) gives
$A_V\le a\le b$. The two Cassini seed inequalities are
\[
                 a(d-\delta)\le bc,\qquad
                 b(d-\delta)\le c^2.                  \tag{7}
\]
Reference C at $N_0$ pays every cumulative read $k\le N_0-3$.
At $k_0=N_0-3$, the ordinary seed is
$F_{k_0}=c$, $F_{k_0-1}=b$, so (7) gives
$F_{k_0}\ge\lambda F_{k_0-1}$.
The same two inequalities seed the earlier D ratios at
$N_0-2,N_0-1$; tail LC supplies all subsequent D ratios needed
through $N$. On this finite increasing prefix,
$F_i=F_{i-1}+D_i$ and $G_i=G_{i-1}+F_i$.
The established finite-read integration lemma now gives
$G_k\ge\lambda G_{k-1}$ for all $k\le N-3$.
This is every C shift. Current decreasing or flat slopes are
automatic. This proves the asserted late-C conclusion without any
early reference hypothesis.

Condition 1 covers the earlier identical reference prefix and
therefore proves complete C for all $u\ge H$. The additional
finite shorter-$u$ tests, with the proved padding and mode bounds,
cover every remaining actual arm. This finishes the finite principle.
