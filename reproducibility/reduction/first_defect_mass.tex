\section{Sharp weight cost of the first missing-hook multiplicity}
Let $X=(x_1\le\cdots\le x_\ell)$ be a nonempty increasing
host of weight $a$, let $\kappa$ be its first missing weight,
and let $d$ be the number of missing compositions of weight
$\kappa$. Thus $d$ is the leading coefficient of $E_X$,
or the last coefficient of its reverse deficit $e_X$.
The first-missing-hook theorem is an established input here.

\begin{theorem}[Exact nonnegative excess decomposition]
Put $n=\kappa-1$, let $t_1,\ldots,t_n$ be the last $n$
capacities of $X$, and let $P$ be the total weight before
these last $n$ positions. Set $\varepsilon=1$ when $\ell=n$
and zero otherwise. Then $t_j\ge j$ and
\[
 d=\varepsilon+\#\{1\le j\le n:t_j=j\},
\]
\[
 a=\frac{\kappa(\kappa-1)}2+\kappa-d
     +(P+\varepsilon-1)
     +\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa-1}\max(t_j-j-1,0).                 \tag{1}
\]
Both terms following $\kappa-d$ are nonnegative. In particular
\[
 1\le d\le\kappa,\qquad
 a\ge\frac{\kappa(\kappa-1)}2+\kappa-d.                    \tag{2}
\]
The bound is sharp for every $\kappa\ge2$ and every
$1\le d\le\kappa$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Universality through weight $n$ gives the final staircase
$t_j\ge j$. At weight $n+1$, the all-ones hook fails
exactly when $\ell=n$, while the hook with first part
$j+1$ fails exactly when $t_j<j+1$, equivalently $t_j=j$.
All missing words at the first missing level are hooks,
which proves the formula for $d$.

For $t_j\ge j$, the point identity
\[
 t_j=j+1-\mathbf1_{\{t_j=j\}}+\max(t_j-j-1,0)
\]
holds. Sum it and add $P$. This proves (1).
If $\ell=n$, then $P=0,\varepsilon=1$.
Otherwise $P\ge1,\varepsilon=0$. Hence $P+\varepsilon-1\ge0$.
The hook count gives $1\le d\le n+1=\kappa$, and (2) follows.

For sharpness, start from $(1,2,\ldots,\kappa-1)$ and
increase any $\kappa-d$ of its capacities by one.
The list remains weakly increasing because adjacent
baseline capacities differ by one. Its last staircase
still embeds every word of weight $\kappa-1$, while
exactly $d$ hooks of weight $\kappa$ fail. Its weight is
the right side of (2).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[All equality hosts]
Equality in (2) holds exactly when all the final staircase
capacities obey $t_j\in\{j,j+1\}$ and $P+\varepsilon=1$.
Equivalently, the host has either $\kappa-1$ positions and
no earlier prefix, or exactly $\kappa$ positions with a
single leading one before its last $\kappa-1$ positions;
in both cases the displayed two-value condition holds.
The first-missing condition excludes the cases with zero
failing hooks.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
All excesses in (1) are nonnegative integers. Equality
forces each to vanish and is conversely sufficient.
The position-count description follows because every
earlier capacity has positive integer weight.
\end{proof}

Thus an actual deficit ending in one has the stronger
mass bound $a\ge\kappa(\kappa-1)/2+\kappa-1$; more
generally every missing terminal unit below $\kappa$
costs at least one extra host weight above the minimal
staircase. This adds a quantitative coupling between
the previously used first-missing mass bound and the
terminal deficit coefficient. It is not a coefficient
unimodality conclusion.

\texttt{FirstDefectMass.lean} kernel-checks the exact
unbounded sum identity, weight bound, equality classification
and hook-count upper bound. The interpretation of the
equal-position count as the actual missing-hook multiplicity
uses the written staircase and first-missing-hook theorems.
