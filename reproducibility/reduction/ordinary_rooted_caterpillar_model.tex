\section{Ordinary reversals are ideal enumerators of explicit rooted trees}
For a finite poset $P$, let
$I(P;z)=\sum_{J\in\mathcal J(P)}z^{|J|}$ enumerate its lower order ideals.
Let $C_d$ be a chain on $d$ elements. Disjoint union multiplies
these polynomials, and adjoining a new element above every element
of a $D-1$ element poset replaces $I$ by $I+z^D$.

Start with $P_0=C_\alpha$. For a formal non-one part $t\ge2$,
define
\[
       P_{Zt}=(P_Z\sqcup C_{t-1})\oplus\{\text{new maximum}\}.
\]
It has $\operatorname{wt}Z+t$ elements. Consequently
\[
 I(P_0;z)=[\alpha+1],\qquad
 I(P_{Zt};z)=[t]I(P_Z;z)+z^{\operatorname{wt}Z+t}.
\]
Induction proves $I(P_X;z)=q_X$ for every normalized formal arm,
without an ordering condition on its capacities.
The cover graph is a tree with a distinguished path through the
successive new maxima; each new maximum has an additional nonempty
chain attached below it. Contracting those attached chains leaves
a binary caterpillar. The initial chain is nonempty.

At the common degree $D=|P_X|$, complement identifies ideals of
$P_X$ with filters, or equivalently ideals of its dual, so
$F_X=I(P_X^{\mathrm{op}};z)$. Products $q_Xq_Y$ are ideal
enumerators of the disjoint union of two explicitly constructed
trees. This retains the precise append source and the capacity
generation that the synthetic scalar profiles omit.

This representation by itself does not supply midpoint log-concavity,
property C, a normalized matching property or the original
one-descent theorem. In particular no theorem for arbitrary rooted
trees may be substituted. For example a rooted star with four
minimal leaves and then a long unary stem above the root has
ideal polynomial
\[
                 (1+z)^4+z^5+z^6+\cdots+z^{20}.
\]
Its LC minor at rank four is $1-4=-3$, strictly before the
degree midpoint. Such a long unary stem is not produced by the
allowed operation: every new maximum in the ordinary construction
has a nonempty side chain ($t\ge2$). The special construction,
rather than just its being a rooted tree, must be used.

\section{Exact low-head capacity constraints beyond the first cover}
Put $r=h+1$, and let $l_0=\alpha+1,l_i=t_i$ be the $r$
interval lengths in the main product of $q_X$.
They are all at least two, and $\sum_i l_i=D+1$.
No proper-source term has degree below three. Therefore, if
$N_s=\#\{i:l_i\ge s\}$,
\[
 q_1=r,\qquad q_2=\binom{h+1}{2}+N_3,\qquad
 0\le N_3\le\min(D-2h-1,h+1).
\]
If $D>2h+1$, then $N_3\ge1$.
This gives the sharp weight-dependent upper bound used in
\texttt{probe\_cover\_q2\_half\_lc\_closure.json}.
At rank three the exact formula is
\[
 q_3=\binom{r+2}{3}-r(r-N_3)-(N_3-N_4)
                +\mathbf1_{\alpha=1,\ t_1=2}.
\]
The indicator is omitted when $h=0$.
These formulas follow by counting degree-two and degree-three
monomials in $\prod_i[l_i]$ and then adding its only possible
degree-three source. They illustrate why a scalar profile can
pass cover count, whole-profile binary domination and Hilbert
growth while still fail the exact capacity constraints.

For instance the synthetic profile
$(1,4,7,8,5,3,2,1,1)$ satisfies those bounds and has
$D=8,h=3,\kappa=3$. The only increasing arms with these weight
and cover parameters are $1^2 2^3$ and $(1,2,2,3)$.
Their reversals are respectively
\[
 (1,4,7,7,5,3,2,1,1),\qquad
 (1,4,7,8,6,4,2,1,1).
\]
Thus the synthetic profile is not actual. Its binary append
has the midpoint triple $(13,8,5)$ and minor $-1$.
The extra actual counts in the second profile come together as
$z^3[3]$; a free singleton increment at degree three is not an
available capacity deformation. This is an obstruction to the
generic scalar closure, not an actual midpoint-LC counterexample.

\section{The smallest remaining first-missing case has just one large jump}
For an increasing normalized arm, the exact recurrence expands to
\[
 \kappa_X=\min\left\{h+2,\ \min_{1\le i\le h}
                         (t_i+1+h-i)\right\}.              \tag{1}
\]
In particular $h\ge\kappa_X-2$ and
\[
 t_i\ge\max(2,\kappa_X-1-h+i),\qquad
 D\ge2h+1+\frac{(\kappa_X-3)(\kappa_X-2)}2.                 \tag{2}
\]
For $\kappa_X\ge3$, the last bound is sharp: equality is attained at
$1,2^{h-\kappa_X+3},3,4,\ldots,\kappa_X-1$.
The ordinary triangular bound is
$\kappa_X(\kappa_X-1)\le2D$; the factor is $\kappa_X-1$,
not $\kappa_X+1$.

For clarity, $\kappa=3$ has a small-count exception: an arm with
just one non-one part has $\kappa=3$ at every capacity.
If there are at least two non-one parts and $\kappa=3$,
the final capacity must be two, so increasing order forces every
non-one part to be two.

Now suppose $h\ge3$, $\kappa_X=4$, and the largest part $L\ge4$.
Deleting that last part leaves first missing weight three.
Its non-one count is at least two, so the preceding observation
forces the entire prefix to be binary. Thus
\[
                         X=1^\alpha2^{h-1}L.              \tag{3}
\]
All assertions are exact consequences of (1), not finite diagnoses.
For a global least counterexample, the existing $h\ge4,L\ge7$
constraints therefore make (3) the only possible owner shape
with first missing weight four. Proving the relevant raw-prefix
or coupled comparison for this unbounded one-jump family would
eliminate the smallest remaining first-missing case. It is not
proved here. Cases with only two non-one parts can have
$\kappa=4$ without a binary prefix and are not included in (3).

\section{A resonant actual family prevents a uniform cover-shifted mode bound}
The sharper skeleton tail bound does not justify moving every
ordinary mode to its main-box center. In fact the gap can be
unbounded even on the first-missing-four one-jump family.

Let $m\ge1$, $Z=(1,2^m)$, $c=2m+1$, and $Y=Z(c+1)$.
The arm $Y$ is increasing, has weight $D=2c+1=4m+3$,
non-one count $H=m+1$, and first missing weight four.
Every coefficient of $q_Z$ is positive on $[0,c]$.
Thus the length-$(c+1)$ window $[c+1]q_Z$ has its unique
global maximum at rank $c$, where it samples the full mass.
Every other window position omits a positive coefficient.
The terminal unit at rank $2c+1$ has coefficient one and
does not change this maximum. Hence
\[
                      \operatorname{firstmode}q_Y=c.
\]
The binary prefix deficit $e_Z$ has support $[0,c-2]$.
Its length-$(c+1)$ window is at its full mass at rank $c$.
The exact append adds one at this same rank:
$e_Y=[c+1]e_Z+z^c$.
All other coefficients of this window are at most its mass,
so $e_Y$ also has a unique maximum at $c$.
In particular
\[
 \operatorname{firstmode}e_Y=\lfloor D/2\rfloor,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{firstmode}q_Y-
          \left\lceil\frac{D-H}{2}\right\rceil
                              =\lfloor m/2\rfloor.         \tag{4}
\]
No fixed additive constant repairs the proposed main-box-center
upper bound on the ordinary first mode. Nor can the universal
half-weight upper bound for the deficit mode be uniformly improved
by a term growing with the cover count.
This does not refute midpoint LC, the sharp skeleton cutoff,
or the original conjecture. It isolates the genuine resonant
point-on-window-plateau mechanism already reflected by the
quasi-center failure at the append $t=c+1$.
