\section{A sharp improved center bound by pairing the first two positive sources}
\begin{theorem}[One-unit improvement, arbitrary number of capacities]
Let $X=1^\alpha(t_1,\ldots,t_h)$ with $\alpha\ge1$, $h\ge2$,
each $t_i\ge2$, and $t_1\le t_2$. The later capacities may have
ANY order. Put $W=\sum_i t_i$, and use
$D_0=D_1=0$, $D_n=(F_n-F_{n-1})^+$ for $n\ge2$.
EVERY nonempty height layer of $D$ has endpoints $L,R$ satisfying
\[
                            L+R\le W+h.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular
\[
                     \ell_D\le\left\lfloor\frac{W+h}{2}\right\rfloor.
                                                               \tag{2}
\]
This improves the previous all-formal-order layer bound $W+h+1$
by one. It is sharp on an unbounded actual family. In particular
it holds for every actual increasing arm with at least two
non-one parts. The initial-one length does not enter the bound.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{An exact positive decomposition, not a sign estimate.}
For $2\le a\le b$ one has
\[
 [b-1]+S_b[a-1]
                  =[a-1][b+1]+z^{a-1}[b-a].              \tag{3}
\]
The last term is zero if $a=b$. Both nonzero terms are symmetric
unimodal CI polynomials. Their endpoint sums are respectively
$a+b-2$ and $a+b-3$, one or two earlier than the old separate
upper-center estimate $a+b-1$ for the left side.

Identity (3) holds as a polynomial over every commutative ring.
For example multiply both sides by $(1-z)^2$ and use
$(1-z)[q]=1-z^q$; both numerators are equal by expansion.
\texttt{SharpenedForwardDerivativeCenters.lean} proves the COMPLETE
identity for arbitrary natural lengths $a-1,b-a$ in every integral
domain, including the equality case and $z=1$. Thus it applies
directly to polynomial rings; it is not a finite coefficient check.

\paragraph{All sizes and every finite initial-one correction.}
The earlier exact derivative decomposition is
\[
 d^\infty=\sum_{i=1}^h[t_i-1]\prod_{j>i}S_{t_j},\qquad
 D_{n+2}=(d^\infty_n-K_n)^+,
 \quad K=z^{\alpha+h-1}\prod_i[t_i].                     \tag{4}
\]
Pair the $i=1,2$ terms using (3), then multiply their two
positive CI pieces by $\prod_{j\ge3}S_{t_j}$.
Their centers are respectively
\[
                       W+h-4,\qquad W+h-5.
\]
Every remaining source, $i\ge3$, has center
$\sum_{j\ge i}t_j+h-i-2\le W+h-4$.
Thus $d^\infty$ is a sum of symmetric unimodal nonnegative
polynomials with endpoint sums at most
\[
                              C=W+h-4.
\]
This is an exact positive expansion for arbitrary $h$, not an
extrapolation from finitely many cases.

For $2n\ge C$, every summand has coefficient at $C-n$ at least
its coefficient at $n$. Hence the same holds for $d^\infty$.
The correction $K$ is symmetric unimodal with center
\[
                2\alpha+W+h-2=C+2\alpha+2>C,
\]
so its reflected inequality goes in the opposite direction.
Subtracting and taking positive parts preserves the upper-center
comparison. The already proved unimodality of positive forward
differences holds for all these formal orders. Reflect a right
height-layer endpoint $R'$ about $C/2$; the same height is
attained at $C-R'$, so its left endpoint satisfies
$L'\le C-R'$. Thus $L'+R'\le C$.
Undoing the shift by two in (4) gives (1), and the first
maximum lies no later than $\lfloor(W+h)/2\rfloor$.
All coefficients are zero-extended, so boundaries cause no exception.

Sharpness already follows from the prior odd uniform triples:
$h=3$, $t_1=t_2=t_3=2m+1\ge3$, $\alpha\ge3t_1$.
Their unique derivative maximum is at $3m+3$, whose maximal
height layer has endpoint sum $6m+6=W+h$.
Thus both (1) and (2) are attained on an unbounded actual family.

The first-two ordering premise cannot be dropped. The FORMAL
unsorted arm $1^2(3,2)$ has derivative maximum two at rank four.
Its maximal layer $[4,4]$ has endpoint sum eight, whereas
$W+h=5+2=7$. Its first mode four also exceeds
$\lfloor(W+h)/2\rfloor=3$. This is not an actual increasing arm
or an original unimodality counterexample. The older $W+h+1$
bound remains valid for arbitrary formal order.

       \quad\hbox{by}\quad 2n\le a+b+c+d+4.              \tag{5}
