\section{Finite initial ones give a decreasing relative derivative loss}
Fix any formal capacity word $U=(t_1,\ldots,t_h)$, $t_i\ge2$,
in arbitrary order. Put $[t]=1+\cdots+z^{t-1}$ and
$S_t=z[t]$. Initial ones always have positive length $\alpha$.
Let $a=d^\infty_U$ be the infinite-initial-ones shifted derivative
from the center theorem, and let $d^\alpha_n=D_{n+2}(1^\alpha U)$.

\begin{theorem}
For every fixed $U$ with $h\ge1$,
\[
             d^\alpha_j a_i\le d^\alpha_i a_j\qquad(i<j).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
Thus $d^\alpha/a$ is nonincreasing on the support of $a$.
In particular its first maximum is no later than the first
maximum of $a$, and every adjacent growth ratio of the actual
positive derivative is bounded by the corresponding ratio of $a$.
Neither ordering of $U$ nor monotonicity of first missing weight
is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exact signed source identity already proved gives
\[
 a=\sum_{i=1}^h A_i,\quad
 A_i=[t_i-1]\prod_{j>i}S_{t_j},\qquad
 K=z^{\alpha+h-1}\prod_{j=1}^h[t_j],\qquad
 d^\alpha=(a-K)^+ .                                           \tag{2}
\]
For each $i$ cancel only the common interval convolution kernel:
\[
 A_i=C_i*[t_i-1],\quad
 K=C_i*B_i,\quad
 C_i=\prod_{j>i}S_{t_j},\quad
 B_i=z^{\alpha+i-1}\prod_{j\le i}[t_j].                        \tag{3}
\]
On the entire support $[0,t_i-2]$ of $[t_i-1]$, the
coefficients of $B_i$ are nondecreasing. Indeed its shift is
nonnegative, and its factor $[t_i]$ cannot have an outgoing
window term in this range. Convolving that factor with the
other nonnegative interval factors therefore gives a cumulative
sum there. Beyond that support the denominator vanishes.
Consequently $B_i/[t_i-1]$ is increasing in the zero-extended,
cross-multiplied likelihood-ratio sense.

Every $C_i$ is a shifted product of interval kernels, hence
has nonnegative order-two Toeplitz minors. Such convolution
preserves the likelihood-ratio order. Applying it to (3)
gives, for all $n<m$,
\[
                         K_n(A_i)_m\le K_m(A_i)_n .            \tag{4}
\]
Sum (4) over $i$ to obtain $K_n a_m\le K_m a_n$.
Subtracting from the common product $a_na_m$ and taking
positive parts proves (1). Zero coefficients cause no hole:
$0\le d^\alpha\le a$, so outside the support all compared
positive derivative coefficients vanish.

The reference $a$ itself is unimodal: taking $\alpha$ larger
than its finite support in (2) realizes it as the entire
positive derivative of a finite formal arm. On and after
the first maximum of this unimodal $a$, the
product of its nonincreasing coefficients with the
nonincreasing density in (1) is nonincreasing. If the maximum
of $d^\alpha$ is positive, it was already attained by that
reference mode; the zero case is immediate. This gives the
mode assertion. The adjacent ratio assertion is just (1).
\end{proof}

This is a comparison to a reference derivative for the SAME
fixed capacity word, stronger than the capacity-independent
center bound. It does not compare the normalized own-cumulative
C margins at different initial-one lengths. Such a monotone
margin assertion is false on actual arms, as recorded previously.
In particular this theorem does not reduce all finite-initial-ones
C comparisons to their infinite-initial-ones limits, and it does
not settle general C or original coefficient unimodality.

\paragraph{A valid positive stage increment, and two invalid stronger readings.}
For sorted capacities the infinite reference does increase
coefficientwise under every legal append. Write
$A_i=d^\infty_{(t_1,\ldots,t_i)}$ and
$B_i=A_i-A_{i-1}$, $A_0=0$.
If the old last capacity is $L$ and the next is $t\ge L$, then
\[
 B_{\rm new}=S_LB_{\rm old}
       +z^{L-1}[t-L](1+z^2A_{\rm old}).
\]
This follows by subtracting
$A_{\rm old}=[L-1]+S_LA_{\rm prev}$ from
$A_{\rm new}=[t-1]+S_tA_{\rm old}$ and using
$S_t-S_L=z^2([t-1]-[L-1])$.
All terms are nonnegative by induction, starting with the
first interval $[t_1-1]$. This also proves nondecreasing
coefficients of the infinite-initial-ones newly added-word
enumerator $1+(S_t-1)F^\infty_U$.

This coefficient order is NOT a likelihood-ratio order in
the append parameter. For $U=2^h$, $h\ge3$, and append three,
the reference pairs at ranks one and two are
$A=(1,2)$ and $A_{\rm new}=(2,2)$; the proposed decreasing
old/new density would require $2\cdot2\le1\cdot2$, which is false.
Nor is the cumulative of the infinite reference always LC:
after this append its cumulative triple is $(3,5,9)$, with
minor $-2$. Equivalently, the previously recorded ordinary
$F-1$ witness $(4,6,10)$ already occurs on a finite prefix
unaffected by initial-one truncation. It therefore also
refutes an infinite-initial-ones LC shortcut. These are not
counterexamples to C or original UI, and no new priority is
claimed for that previously recorded scalar witness.
