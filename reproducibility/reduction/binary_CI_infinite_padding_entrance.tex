\section{An unbounded infinite-padding CI entrance by geometric deficits}
This note proves an infinite-padding coefficient-LC statement and its
specified large-finite-padding corollary. The remaining smaller paddings,
the generic two-seed conjecture, general C, general A2 and the original
one-descent conjecture are NOT concluded by this note.

Write $q=\phi^{-1}=\phi-1$, so $q^2+q=1$ and
$3/5<q<2/3$. All negative coefficient indices are zero.
For $u\ge0$ define the degree-$u$ polynomial
\[
 Q_u(z)=\sum_{n=0}^u [z^n]\frac{(1+z)^u}{1+qz}\,z^n.
\]
Its leading coefficient is $(1-q)^u$, and
\[
 Q_u=(1+z)Q_{u-1}-q(1-q)^{u-1}z^u.                 \tag{1}
\]
Thus its coefficients are strictly positive and ULC of order $u$:
induct using preservation of ULC orders under convolution, and observe
that lowering ONLY the leading coefficient preserves normalized LC.
The external input here is precisely finite-order ULC convolution
(Liggett 1997; Gurvits, Theorem 1.1,
\texttt{https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.1181}). It is applied only to
$Q_{u-1}$ and $(1+z)$, not to an actual arm or an interval product.

For $n\le u-1$ the positive expansion
\[
 (Q_u)_n=\sum_{j=0}^n {u-1-j\choose n-j}(1-q)^j       \tag{2}
\]
gives $(Q_u)_n\ge {u-1\choose n}$ and
\[
 \frac{(Q_u)_k}{(Q_u)_{k-1}}\ge\frac{u-k}{k}
                  \quad(1\le k\le u-1).             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, division of (2) by ${u-1\choose n}$ gives a sum
of nonnegative multiples of $(n)_j/(u-1)_j$, nondecreasing in $n$.
ULC also gives, with $r_k=(Q_u)_k/(Q_u)_{k-1}$,
\[
 \frac{r_{s+1}}{r_k}\le
 \frac{k(u-s)}{(s+1)(u-k+1)}\quad(1\le k\le s<u).
                                                               \tag{4}
\]
Moreover, for $u\ge3$,
\[
 (Q_u)_s\ge u-q\quad(1\le s\le u-2).                \tag{5}
\]
To check the right endpoint, the two last coefficient recurrences give
$(Q_u)_{u-2}-(u-q)=uq-2+(1-q)^u/q^2$. At $u=3$ this is zero;
for $u\ge4$ its first two terms are already positive.
Ordinary LC puts every intervening coefficient above the smaller
endpoint. No symmetry of $Q_u$ is asserted.

\begin{lemma}[A quantitative binomial-division kernel]
Put $Q=Q_u$ and $T_k=Q_{k+1}+2qQ_k+q^2Q_{k-1}$.
For $u\ge3$, $2\le s\le u-2$, and $1\le k\le s$,
\[
 Q_sQ_k-Q_{s+1}Q_{k-1}\ge q^sT_k.                  \tag{6}
\]
Also, if $0\le a\le(u-1)/2$,
\[
              Q_s\ge q^s(T_0+a)\quad(2\le s\le u-2).
                                                               \tag{7}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By (4), the left side of (6) is at least
$Q_sQ_k/(s+1)$. By (3)--(4), for $1\le k\le u-2$,
\[
 \frac{T_k}{Q_k}\le
 \frac{u-q}{2}+2q+\frac{q^2(u-2)}2\le u+q.          \tag{8}
\]
The last inequality has doubled margin $q(u-3)+2$.
For $s=2,u\ge6$, use $Q_s\ge(u-1)(u-2)/2$ and
$q^2(u+q)\le(2/5)(u+1)\le(u-1)(u-2)/6$.
For $s=3,u\ge6$, use
$Q_s\ge {u-1\choose3}\ge(u-1)(u-2)/2$ and
$q^3(u+q)\le(4/15)(u+1)\le(u-1)(u-2)/8$.
These two elementary quadratic inequalities hold at six and their
left-minus-right differences increase thereafter.

For $s\ge4$, $(s+1)q^s$ is nonincreasing and is at most
$5q^4\le4/5$. Since $u\ge6\ge9q$, (5) yields
$Q_s\ge u-q\ge(4/5)(u+q)\ge(s+1)q^s(u+q)$.
This proves (6), apart from the following SEVEN exact small cases.
The table records the margin in the basis $(1,\phi)$; every entry
is positive (already $\phi<5/3$ suffices).
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|r}
u&s&k&Q_sQ_k-Q_{s+1}Q_{k-1}-q^sT_k\\\hline
4&2&1&44-22\phi\\
4&2&2&62-34\phi\\
5&2&1&70-30\phi\\
5&2&2&120-60\phi\\
5&3&1&165-90\phi\\
5&3&2&280-155\phi\\
5&3&3&210-120\phi
\end{array}
\]
Finally $T_0=u+q$. For (7), use (5), $q^s\le q^2$,
and $a\le(u-1)/2$. Twice the remaining margin is
$(3q-1)u+3-7q$, which at $u\ge4$ is at least $5q-1>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Robust LC budget after binomial division]
Let $L_0=1$, let $L$ have positive LC coefficients and interval
support, and suppose $a=L_1\le(u-1)/2$, $u\ge3$.
Put $B=LQ_u$. For every $1\le s\le u-2$,
\[
 B_s^2-B_{s-1}B_{s+1}\ge
 q^s(B_{s+1}+2qB_s+q^2B_{s-1}).                    \tag{9}
\]
No CI factorization of $L$ is needed for this theorem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $s=1$, use $L_2\le a^2$ and the exact first two coefficients
$B_1=u+a-q$,
$B_2={u\choose2}+ua+L_2-q(u+a)+q^2$.
Twice the margin in (9) is at least
\[
 f(a)=q^2u(u-1)+2a(q^2u-qa-1).
\]
This is concave on $0\le a\le(u-1)/2$. Its endpoint values are
$f(0)\ge0$ and
\[
 f((u-1)/2)=\frac{u-1}{2}\{(4-5q)u+q-2\}>0,
\]
where $4-5q\ge2/3$ and $u\ge3$.

For $s\ge2$, apply Cauchy--Binet to the $2$ by $2$
Toeplitz minor of $B=LQ_u$. Every term is nonnegative since both
factors are LC. Retain just the terms with the first intermediate
row zero. With $K(s,k)=Q_sQ_k-Q_{s+1}Q_{k-1}$, this gives
\[
 B_s^2-B_{s-1}B_{s+1}\ge\sum_{i=0}^s L_iK(s,s-i).
\]
Use (6) on $i<s$, and (7) on $i=s$.
Since $L_{s+1}\le aL_s$ by LC (including zero extension), the
retained terms pay the entire convolution with $T$, including its
otherwise missing $i=s+1$ term $L_{s+1}T_{-1}=L_{s+1}$.
This is exactly (9). All omitted terms were nonnegative.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every CI tail, infinite padding, unbounded degree]
Let $L=\prod_{t\in V}[t]$, all $t\ge3$, with degree
$h=\sum(t-1)\ge2$. For EVERY $m\ge h$, put
\[
 C=L\sum_{j=0}^m(z+z^2)^j.
\]
Then the ordinary coefficients of $C$ are LC at EVERY centre
$n\ge m$. There is no bound on tail count, capacities or degree.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $u=m+1$ and $a_*=L(-\phi)$. Each factor satisfies
\[
 |[t](-\phi)|q^{t-1}
       =|(-1)^tq-q^{t+1}|\le q+q^4=2q^2<1.
\]
Consequently $|a_*|q^m\le1$. Also $L_1=|V|\le h/2\le(u-1)/2$,
so (9) applies to $B=LQ_u$.

Define the signed geometric deficit $b_n=\phi C_{n-1}-C_n$.
Factoring $1-z-z^2=(1-\phi z)(1+qz)$ gives, EXACTLY,
\[
 b_n=B_{n-m-1}-a_*(-q)^n
           \quad(h\le n\le m+1+u).                 \tag{10}
\]
Here only the indices $n=m+1,\ldots,m+h+1$ are needed.
The formula follows by dividing
$(1-z-z^2)C=L-z^{m+1}(1+z)^{m+1}L$ by $1+qz$;
the initial $L$ term equals $a_*(-q)^n$ already at $n=h$.
The geometric-series tail of $Q_u$ starts at index $u+1$,
so it does not affect any index used in (10).

The deficits $b_n$ are positive on $m+1\le n\le m+h+1$:
$B_0=1$, the perturbation has magnitude at most $q$, and for
$1\le s\le h\le u-1$ one has $B_s\ge Q_s>0$;
at $s=u-1$ use $Q_{u-1}=(1-(1-q)^u)/q>1$.
For centres $m+2\le n\le m+h$, put $s=n-m-1$.
Expansion gives
\[
 b_n^2-b_{n-1}b_{n+1}
 =B_s^2-B_{s-1}B_{s+1}
   +a_*(-q)^{n-1}(B_{s+1}+2qB_s+q^2B_{s-1})\ge0,   \tag{11}
\]
by (9) and $|a_*|q^{n-1}\le q^s$.

The binary polynomial in $C$ is already LC from centre $m$
by the proved ordinary binary-tail theorem. Truncate its coefficients
below $m-1$ to zero, obtaining a globally LC sequence. Its convolution
with $L$ agrees with every original triple from centre $m+h$ onward.
Ordinary LC convolution therefore gives LC of $C$ from there onward.
To move back across the remaining finite band, write
$M_n=C_n^2-C_{n-1}C_{n+1}$ and use the EXACT identity
\[
 C_n(b_{n+1}^2-b_nb_{n+2})+b_nM_{n+1}
                           =\phi b_{n+1}M_n.        \tag{12}
\]
Positive deficits and (11) allow backward induction from $m+h$
down to $m+1$. At the last centre $m$, the previously proved
first-defect Cassini payment applies: its physical centre is
$m+|V|+2\ge W+2$. Even phase has a positive Cassini term;
odd phase starts at $W+3$ and is paid by $r_{W+2}\ge A_V$.
The first infinite-padding defect is one. This gives $M_m\ge0$.
No infinite backward induction and no assertion about earlier
coefficient centres is required.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Actual scope and remaining gap.}
By the exact signed-tail identification, this proves LC of the
infinite-initial-run actual positive slopes from physical centre
$m+|V|+2$ when $u=m+1\ge h+1$. At the single entrance $u=h+1$
this starts at $W+2$, precisely the required ordinary LC band.
It supplies the large-padding representatives whose positive slopes
agree with the infinite-padding arm through the first mode.
The finite-padding term $-z^{m+\alpha}(1+z)^{m+1}L$ remains to
be handled for the other representatives. In particular this does
NOT discharge the all-padding premise of the finite principle.

\begin{corollary}[Only the first half of the padding representatives remain]
At the entrance $u=h+1$, the required ordinary positive-slope LC
band holds for EVERY $\alpha\ge h+1$, without any finite check.
Consequently, the all-padding entrance premise in the proved finite
principle reduces from $\alpha=1,\ldots,2h+2$ to JUST
$\alpha=1,\ldots,h$.
More generally, for $m=u-1\ge h$, LC at the MOVING centres
$u+|V|+1\le n<\ell$ holds whenever
\[
               \alpha\ge\left\lceil\frac{m+h}{2}\right\rceil+1.
                                                               \tag{13}
\]
Together with the previously proved first-defect and integration
payments, this gives actual late property C at every $N\ge W+1$
for all these parameters. Early reference C and smaller paddings
are not asserted here.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each term $z^k(1+z)^k L$ in $C$ is symmetric and unimodal about
$(3k+h)/2$. Therefore the first mode of their sum is at most
$\lceil(3m+h)/2\rceil$. The same upper bound, shifted by $r+2$,
holds for the infinite-padding positive-slope first mode: its
entire tail from $n\ge h-1$ is exactly $C$, so an earlier larger
value can only move the first global mode further left.
Under (13), the subtracted coefficient term begins at
$m+\alpha>\lceil(3m+h)/2\rceil$. Thus the actual positive
slopes agree with their infinite-padding values through that
global first mode, and everywhere else are bounded above by them.
Their global maximum and its first location are unchanged.
Every triple with centre STRICTLY before that mode agrees with
the already proved LC infinite-padding triple.
The reference-prefix C band and the subsequent finite integration
proof in the sharpened finite principle now apply to this particular
actual arm directly; no induction over the unhandled paddings is used.
At $m=h$ condition (13) is just $\alpha\ge h+1$.
\end{proof}
